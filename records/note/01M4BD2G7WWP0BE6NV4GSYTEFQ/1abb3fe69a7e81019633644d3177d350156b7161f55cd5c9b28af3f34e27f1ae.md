# 05 — Selection and promotion: how a toolset evolves by use

Round 2, iteration 5, track "selection" (see `STATE.md`, "Runde 2"). The new premise: tools and
workflows are cheap to make, are generated per problem and are mostly thrown away. **The standard
is what lasts.** The toolset should grow through *use and selection*: "if they can be combined,
their use grows." The problem record is the spine. Every record is `about: <problem>`, and an
outcome record judges the result against the problem's success criteria.

This track answers one question: **given cheap, disposable tools, what decides which ones survive,
and how does the system get better at the next problem?**

Researched 2026-10-07. Sources are listed in §9. *(unverified)* marks claims taken from secondary
sources or from background knowledge that was not re-checked this session.

---

## 0. The design fact that shapes everything: N is small

The famous selection systems all work on large populations:
- npm has millions of packages and billions of downloads;
- app stores have millions of ratings;
- MAP-Elites runs thousands of evaluations per niche;
- Voyager and the Darwin Gödel Machine run hundreds of iterations against an automatic judge.

This system has **one user** (04 §8.1: personal toolchain first), maybe 5–50 problems a month, and
no automatic judge of "solved". Two consequences follow:

1. **Usage counts are close to worthless as signals.** At this N a tool used 12 times might have
   been used 12 times in one problem by one runaway chain. The strong signals are the few **human
   acts**: accept, reject, the edit before accept, and the outcome verdict. There are maybe tens of
   them a month. The design spends them carefully and never dilutes them with counts.
2. **The large ecosystems teach mostly by warning.** npm shows that popularity is not quality
   (trivial packages are "deployment tested" but under-tested, §9). ClawHub shows that an open
   library without curation fills with junk and malware (§9). The ideas we *can* borrow are
   structural: Debian's age + no-regression gate, Kubernetes' tiered deprecation, atproto's
   additive schemas, MAP-Elites' niches, Voyager's verified skill library and Agent Workflow
   Memory's induced workflows.

A third fact follows from round 1. **Authority stays where 04 put it.** The vocabulary is closed
(`approve`, `accept`, later `grant`), only a human makes anything authoritative, and only
deterministic $0 steps run unattended (04 §8.4). Selection must fit inside that rule. It needs
**no new authority verb**. Promotion, merge and deprecation are `decision` records
`about: <tool family>`. The system may *propose* them, and only a human `accept` over exact hashes
makes them real.

## 1. Units of selection and their identity

Selection needs stable things to select. A regenerated tool gets a new hash every time, so hash
alone cannot carry a track record. Three levels are used, all from the existing envelope (`id` is
the sha256, `subject` is a stable logical id, `prev` chains revisions, `links.derivedFrom` gives
lineage; 01-data-model §3.3, 02-B §4.1):

| Unit | Identity | What selection acts on |
|---|---|---|
| **Variant** | `id` (sha256 of the tool definition) | What runs. Execution and every provenance stamp always pin the exact hash. |
| **Family** | `subject: tool/<slug>` with variants chained by `prev`, and forks/regenerations by `derivedFrom` | What earns a track record and gets promoted, merged or deprecated. |
| **Niche** | `interface` signature: `reads[types] → writes[types]`, plus a domain label | What competes with what. Two families compete only inside a niche (MAP-Elites, §4.4). |

The same three levels apply to **chains** (a chain variant is a recipe hash, a chain family is a
preset subject, a chain niche is a problem shape; §5) and to **types** (§4.5).

### 1.1 The tool definition record

```json
{
  "kind": "doc", "type": "tool.def",
  "subject": "tool/extract-decisions", "prev": "sha256:aa01…",
  "links": [
    { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:aa01…" },          // parent variant (regenerated or edited)
    { "rel": "about",       "to": "problem:01JA3…" }         // the problem it was generated for
  ],
  "body": {
    "blurb": "Reads a research synthesis; proposes one decision per recommendation.",
    "interface": { "reads": ["doc"], "writes": ["decision:proposed"] },
    "entry": { "cmd": "bin/extract-decisions", "blob": "sha256:7c2e…" },
    "fixtures": ["sha256:f1…", "sha256:f2…"],                 // optional, see §4.2 "golden pairs"
    "cost": "one LLM call per input doc"
  },
  "prov": { "principal": "agent:run:01JA3…", "module": "foundry@0.1", "reads": ["…"], "…": "…" }
}
```

**Notation.** JSON blocks in this document are JSONC: `//` comments are annotations. Some links
point at a **subject** (`problem:01JA3…`, `tool/<slug>`), not a sha256. That is a **proposed
envelope change** for the invariants track. 04 §2 says the kernel owns `links` *as sha256*, and in
02-B `about` targets a hash. The proposal:
- `about`, and references to a family or preset, may name a subject, because they mean "this
  logical thing, whichever revision";
- `derivedFrom`, `supersedes` on records, and anything a human act names stay sha256.

Selection needs both. Credit flows over hashes, and track records accumulate on subjects.

`blurb` is deliberately **not** a selection input. It is the part an agent can most easily
optimize to look good (§3.4).

## 2. Lifecycle

```
            (generated per problem)
                   │
                   ▼
   ┌──────────► scratch ──── TTL with no credit ────► forgotten (out of the index; records stay)
   │               │
   │      used with credit in a 2nd distinct problem   [automatic, $0]
   │               ▼
   │             kept ──────── TTL with no credit ───► forgotten
   │               │
   │      human accepts a `tool.tier` decision          [system proposes, human decides]
   │               ▼
   │            library ────── superseded / no use ───► deprecated ──► (still runnable by hash, never
   │                                                                     proposed, never deleted)
   └─── a library tool forked for a new problem starts its own variant as scratch, `derivedFrom` it
```

### 2.1 Transitions, triggers and who decides

| Transition | Trigger (all computable from records) | Decided by | Why that party |
|---|---|---|---|
| → **scratch** | A run writes a `tool.def`. | Automatic. | It costs nothing. Scratch tools are only visible inside their own problem. |
| scratch → **kept** | The family has `credit > 0` (§3.2) in **≥ 2 distinct problems**, and in at least one of them it was *not* the problem it was generated for. | Automatic, deterministic, $0. | It only changes what proposers may *suggest*. It is reversible and grants no authority. This is the "used once → reused" step. |
| kept → **library** | The system proposes a `tool.tier` decision when the family meets the bar (§2.2). | **Human `accept`** over the exact variant hash. | Library tools are offered first in every new problem. That is influence over future spend, so it follows the trust rule. |
| library → **deprecated** | (a) A merge supersedes it (§4.3), (b) a better variant in the same niche wins ≥ 3 of the last 4 direct comparisons (§3.5), or (c) no credit in 90 days. | System proposes, **human accepts**. | Deprecation changes what runs next, and a human promoted it. Symmetric authority. |
| scratch/kept → **forgotten** | No credit for N days (default: 30 for scratch, 90 for kept). | Automatic, $0. | Forgetting drops a family from the proposer's index and from views. **No record is deleted**: provenance chains that point at the hash still resolve. Biology's apoptosis, not extinction. |
| deprecated → (nothing) | — | — | Deprecated tools stay runnable by hash forever (old chains replay). They are never proposed. This is the Kubernetes GA rule: deprecate, don't remove (§9). |

### 2.2 The library bar

Promotion is Debian's testing gate rather than npm's popularity contest. Britney migrates a
package from unstable to testing only after an **age** and only with **no new release-critical
bugs** (§9). Translated:

- **Age:** the family has existed ≥ 14 days and was used in ≥ 3 distinct problems.
- **No regression:** no `reject` of its output and no outcome verdict `failed` attributed to it in
  the last 2 uses.
- **Credit, niche-relative:** survival and precision at or above the **median of its niche**
  (§4.4), not above an absolute cutoff. Survival applies only where the family's outputs have
  accepted descendants (§3.3). An upstream family (research, retrieval) whose outputs are consumed
  rather than accepted is judged on its count of credited paths instead. Without this, any tool 3–4
  hops from the outcome could never be promoted. γ³ ≈ 0.34 before fan-in, survival n = 0, and a
  source-gatherer's precision is naturally low. That would contradict 04's choice of research as
  the first module.
- **A golden pair exists:** at least one accepted (input → output) pair that can serve as its
  regression test (§4.2).

The thresholds are defaults chosen to be reachable in a month of personal use. They should be
tuned from the friction log, not argued in advance.

### 2.3 The promotion decision record

```json
{
  "kind": "decision", "type": "tool.tier", "status": "proposed",
  "subject": "tier/tool/extract-decisions",
  "links": [
    { "rel": "about",       "to": "tool/extract-decisions" },
    { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<fitness fold snapshot>" }
  ],
  "body": {
    "tier": "library",
    "pin": "sha256:c31f…",                       // the exact variant promoted
    "evidence": { "problems": 4, "survival": 0.81, "precision": 0.42, "goldenPairs": 3 },
    "replaces": []                               // or families this supersedes
  }
}
```

The human's `accept` names `sha256:c31f…`. That variant is promoted, not "the latest". It is the
same rule as swarm's approve-by-`snapshotSha` (D-014), which is the lesson from kernel A's failure
(04 §3).

## 3. Fitness signals

### 3.1 The raw facts (nothing new needs to be written)

Every signal below is a **fold over records that already exist** in the round-1 design, plus the
two records the new premise adds (`problem` and `outcome`):

| Fact | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Which variant produced a record | `prov.module` / `prov.run` (kernel-stamped in B; self-reported in C, see §8) |
| Which problem a run served | `about: problem:<ulid>` on the run's trigger, inherited like B's labels (02-B §1, "labels inherit from the trigger") |
| What a record was built from | `links.derivedFrom` (B checks `derivedFrom ⊆ prov.reads`) |
| What the human made authoritative | human acts: `accept`, `reject`, `supersede` over exact hashes |
| What the human changed first | a `supersedes`/`prev` chain from a tool's output to the revision the human accepted |
| Whether the problem was solved | the `outcome` record (§3.6) |
| What it cost | `prov.cost` |

**Credit uses `derivedFrom`, not `prov.reads`.** The reads-set says what a run *fetched*. A run
that reads 40 records and uses two would otherwise credit all 40. `derivedFrom` is the producer's
statement of what it *used*. B makes it a subset of the reads-set, so it cannot cite what it never
saw. It can still over-cite, which §3.4 handles.

### 3.2 Credit assignment along a chain

**Sinks carry value. Everything else earns value only by flowing into a sink.**

Sink values (only human-authoritative records are sinks):

| Sink | Value |
|---|---|
| `outcome` accepted, verdict `solved` | 1.0 |
| `outcome` accepted, verdict `partial` | 0.5 × (criteria met / criteria total) |
| `decision` accepted (any type except `tool.tier` and `outcome`) | 0.3 |
| `outcome` accepted, verdict `failed` | 0 credit, and it is recorded as a **blame** fact against every family in the lineage (used by the no-regression gate, not as negative credit) |

**Meta-records are never sinks.** Records *about* tools, types, chains or selection itself are
excluded: `tool.tier`, `tool.merge`, `type.tier`, `choice`, `chain.preset` revisions, `lesson`,
`tool.golden` and the fitness fold. Otherwise the proposer, merger and retrospective modules would
earn credit every time the human accepts their proposals. That is the Goodhart loop of §3.4,
applied to the selector itself. Selection modules are judged only indirectly, by whether the
problems that used their proposals got solved.

Propagation is a reverse walk over the `derivedFrom` DAG:

```
credit(sink)      = value(sink) × survival(sink)
for each record r with credit c and parents P = derivedFrom(r):
    each parent p receives  c × γ / |P|          γ = 0.7 per hop
credit(family F, problem X) = Σ credit of records produced by variants of F, inside X's lineage
```

- **Divide by |P| (fan-in).** A record citing ten parents does not hand full credit to all ten.
  Over-citing dilutes and does not amplify.
- **γ per hop.** A tool five hops upstream gets 0.17 of the credit of the last step. That is
  roughly the honest uncertainty about its contribution.
- **No credit through the same family.** If family F's output is `derivedFrom` another output of
  F, the edge carries no credit to F. That blocks self-reinforcing loops (the critic-loop case,
  C3 in 02).
- **Cap at 1 per problem per family.** No amount of activity inside one problem earns more than
  "this family was fully responsible for solving this problem".

This is a simple, deterministic stand-in for Shapley-style attribution. The honest version is a
counterfactual ("would it have been solved without F?"). A counterfactual costs a re-run, so it
belongs in direct comparisons (§3.5), which are opt-in and budgeted.

### 3.3 Survival: did the human keep it or rewrite it?

The strongest cheap signal at small N is **how much of a tool's output survives into what the human
accepted.**

```
survival(r) = 1 − normEditDistance(body(r), body(accepted descendant of r on the prev/supersedes chain))
            = 1   when accepted unchanged
            = 0   when rejected, or rewritten past recognition
```

- Survival is **defined only where an accepted descendant exists** (decisions, drafts, outcomes).
  For outputs that are consumed rather than accepted (sources, findings, indexes), it is undefined
  (n = 0), and the family is judged on credited paths instead (§2.2).
- It needs no new data. The `prev` chain from a proposed decision to its accepted revision already
  exists, and so does a draft's chain to its published revision.
- Use token-level edit distance on the body's text fields, or field-level equality for structured
  bodies (status, relation and choice must match, wording may differ).
- **A rewrite is information, not just a penalty.** The diff between what the tool wrote and what
  the human accepted is the best training data the system will ever have for the next variant.
  The foundry track (05-foundry) should feed it to regeneration as "what the human changed last
  time".

### 3.4 Anti-Goodhart: what must not count, and why

Goodhart's law: a measure that becomes a target stops being a good measure (§9, background). The
target here is "be proposed again", and three parties could game it: the generating agent, the
proposing agent, and the human's own fatigue.

| Gaming pattern | Signal it would inflate | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| **Flooding.** A tool emits 50 records hoping some get used. | usage counts, raw credit | Credit only flows from sinks. Also report **precision = records on a credited path / records emitted**. A low-precision tool costs human attention even if it scores. |
| **Over-citing.** An output lists every input in `derivedFrom` so credit spreads to friends. | upstream credit | The fan-in divisor (§3.2). Optionally check that a cited parent's text overlaps the output. |
| **Sibling reuse.** One chain calls the tool 30 times in one problem. | reuse count | Reuse counts **distinct problem ULIDs**, never runs. |
| **Home-problem reuse.** It is "reused" only in follow-ups of the problem it was made for. | reuse | Weight reuse by **problem distance**: reuse in a problem with no shared `about` ancestor and no shared labels counts 1.0, and reuse in a child problem counts 0.3. This is exaptation, a trait selected in a context it wasn't built for, and it is the best evidence of generality. |
| **Rich get richer.** The proposer offers the top tool, so it gets used, so it stays top (Matthew effect). | everything | **Exploration quota:** proposals use Thompson sampling (§3.5), which keeps offering under-tried variants in proportion to their uncertainty. |
| **Rubber-stamping.** The human batch-accepts 20 decisions at 23:00. | survival, accept rate | Record batch size on the human act. An accept from a batch of more than 5, made with no edits, counts at half weight for survival. That is not distrust of the human. A batch accept is simply less evidence. 04 §8.4 allows batch accepts deliberately. This rule leaves them fully valid as authority and only discounts them as *evidence*. |
| **Description optimization.** The generator writes a glowing `blurb`. | proposer choice | `blurb` is not an input to fitness. The proposer shows track-record numbers next to it. |
| **One scalar.** Any single number becomes the target. | — | **Fitness is a vector**: shown as a vector, ranked lexicographically, never summed. |

### 3.5 Choosing among variants: Thompson sampling plus direct comparison

At this N the right statistical tool is a small Bayesian bandit, not a leaderboard.

- Each family in a niche keeps `Beta(α, β)`. `α = 1 + uses on a credited path to an accepted solved
  or partial outcome`, so any credit > 0 counts as a success, whatever the hop distance. Credit
  *magnitude* stays a separate field in the fitness vector. `β = 1 + uses with blame`, plus uses with
  survival < 0.3 where survival is defined.
- To propose a tool for a niche, sample once from each family's Beta and offer the highest sample,
  plus the second highest when the samples are close. Untried variants start at Beta(1,1) and so
  get offered sometimes. Proven ones get offered most of the time. **This is the exploration
  quota without a tunable quota.**
- **Direct comparison (opt-in, budgeted).** When two variants compete for a niche, the system may
  propose a *shadow run*: both variants run on the same inputs, and the human sees the outputs
  side by side, unlabeled, and picks one. The pick is a `choice` record. It is a pairwise
  preference (Bradley-Terry). It is the only cheap counterfactual available, and it costs one
  extra run, so it is a proposal with a budget and never runs on its own (04 §8.4).

### 3.6 The problem and outcome records

The selection loop has nothing to aim at without them. This track needs exactly these fields.
The invariants track owns the final shape.

```json
{ "kind": "doc", "type": "problem",
  "subject": "problem:01JA3…",
  "links": [ { "rel": "about", "to": "problem:01J9X…" } ],          // parent problem, optional
  "body": {
    "statement": "How should square store saves and missions?",
    "successCriteria": [
      { "id": "c1", "text": "A decision exists that names a storage format", "check": "human" },
      { "id": "c2", "text": "A migration path for existing saves",          "check": "human" },
      { "id": "c3", "text": "swarm feature builds with basis pinned",       "check": "auto:swarm.merged" }
    ],
    "labels": { "domain": ["storage", "game"], "project": "square" },
    "budget": { "usd": 20 }
  } }
```

```json
{ "kind": "decision", "type": "outcome", "status": "proposed",      // human accept makes it real
  "subject": "outcome/problem:01JA3…",
  "links": [ { "rel": "about", "to": "problem:01JA3…" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<decision D-…>" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<swarm merge artifact>" } ],
  "body": {
    "verdict": "solved",                                  // solved | partial | failed | abandoned
    "criteria": [ { "id": "c1", "met": "yes", "evidence": ["sha256:…"] },
                  { "id": "c2", "met": "partial", "evidence": [] },
                  { "id": "c3", "met": "yes", "evidence": ["sha256:…"] } ],
    "cost": { "usd": 11.40, "humanActs": 9, "wallDays": 3 },
    "note": "Migration left as a follow-up problem."
  } }
```

- The **outcome is a `decision` kind** so it inherits the human-only status lattice. An agent can
  draft the verdict but cannot accept it. Otherwise the whole fitness system would rest on an
  agent grading its own work.
- `derivedFrom` on the outcome names the records that **were the solution**. That is where the
  credit walk (§3.2) starts. The human prunes this list when accepting, and that pruning is the
  single most valuable credit-assignment act in the system.
- `abandoned` carries no blame. Problems can stop mattering.

### 3.7 The fitness fold (derived view, regenerated, never truth)

Mutable state is always a fold (01-data-model, implications 2). Fitness is a view rebuilt from
the log by a deterministic $0 module. It is never edited and never authoritative.

```json
{
  "family": "tool/extract-decisions",
  "niche": { "reads": ["doc"], "writes": ["decision:proposed"], "domain": "research" },
  "tier": "kept",                                   // from the latest accepted tool.tier, else computed
  "asOf": "2026-10-07T14:00Z", "logHead": "sha256:…",
  "problems":   { "distinct": 4, "weightedByDistance": 2.6, "home": "problem:01JA3…" },
  "credit":     { "total": 1.92, "perProblem": { "problem:01JA3…": 0.71, "…": "…" } },
  "survival":   { "mean": 0.81, "n": 11 },          // §3.3
  "precision":  { "credited": 9, "emitted": 21, "ratio": 0.43 },
  "blame":      [ { "problem": "problem:01JB…", "outcome": "sha256:…" } ],
  "beta":       { "alpha": 4, "beta": 2 },          // §3.5
  "combinability": { "consumedBy": 3, "consumes": 1 },   // §3.8
  "cost":       { "usdPerCreditedUse": 0.18 },
  "lastCredit": "2026-10-05",
  "goldenPairs": ["sha256:gp1…", "sha256:gp2…"]     // §4.2
}
```

### 3.8 Combinability as a fitness axis

The owner's sentence "if they can be combined, their use grows" makes combinability a fitness
dimension of its own, not a by-product.

- `consumedBy` = the number of **distinct other families** whose credited outputs are
  `derivedFrom` this family's outputs. `consumes` is the reverse.
- A tool that reads and writes **shared** types (§4.5) can sit in more chains. A tool whose output
  type only it understands is a dead end however good it is.
- It is not a hard filter, because specialists are fine. It is a lexicographic tie-breaker and a
  promotion argument: "promoting this also promotes `research.finding` to a shared type, which
  three other families already write."

## 4. Dedup, convergence and the evolution of types

### 4.1 Finding near-duplicates (automatic, $0 first)

Cheap generation will produce five `extract-decisions`. Finding them is a ladder from free to paid:

1. **Same niche** (equal interface signature) and an overlapping `domain` label. Deterministic, $0.
   This finds most of them.
2. **Same behavior on recorded inputs.** The store already holds every input each variant ever
   ran on, by hash. Re-running variant A on B's recorded inputs and comparing outputs is the real
   test of sameness. It costs money, so it is a proposal.
3. **Embedding similarity of definitions**, only to rank pairs when step 1 returns too many. Local
   embeddings are nearly free, but the step is not deterministic, so it ranks and never decides.

ToolLibGen, ToolScope and El Agente Forjador all use (3) as the first step: cluster by semantic
similarity, then let an agent merge (§9). The ordering here is deliberate. We have something those
systems lack, a typed interface, so the free deterministic check comes first.

### 4.2 Golden pairs: the regression corpus provenance gives for free

A **golden pair** is `(input hashes, output hash)` where the output was accepted by a human with
survival ≥ 0.9. It is a fold over existing records, and nobody writes tests by hand.

```json
{ "kind": "doc", "type": "tool.golden",
  "links": [ { "rel": "about", "to": "tool/extract-decisions" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<accepted output>" } ],
  "body": { "inputs": ["sha256:…"], "expected": "sha256:<accepted output>",
            "compare": "fields:[status,choice,relations]",      // what must match; prose may differ
            "fromProblem": "problem:01JA3…" } }
```

Golden pairs are what make a merge safe, and they are the "no regression" half of the library
bar (§2.2). They also give regenerated tools a test suite drawn from real use, which Voyager's
self-verification and SkillWeaver's "practice" approximate with an LLM judge (§9).

### 4.3 Merging

A merge is a **new family**, not an edit of the old ones:

```json
{ "kind": "decision", "type": "tool.merge", "status": "proposed",
  "links": [ { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<pinned variant of extract-decisions>" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<pinned variant of decisions-from-synthesis>" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<pinned variant of rec-to-decision>" } ],
  "body": {
    "into": "sha256:<new tool.def>",
    "goldenResult": { "passed": 14, "failed": 1, "failedPairs": ["sha256:gp7…"] },
    "supersedes": ["tool/extract-decisions", "tool/decisions-from-synthesis", "tool/rec-to-decision"],
    "costUsd": 0.90
  } }
```

- The merged variant must pass the **union of the parents' golden pairs**. Each failure is listed,
  and the human sees exactly which accepted behavior would change. This is ToolLibGen's "reviewing
  agent ensures the aggregate keeps the full functionality" (§9), with a deterministic check in
  place of a reviewing agent.
- On accept, the parents become `deprecated` (§2.1). The merged family **inherits the parents'
  track record** through `derivedFrom`, so it is not punished as a newcomer. The parents' Beta
  parameters are summed.
- Old records keep pointing at old hashes. Nothing is migrated. Replaying an old chain runs the old
  variant.
- **Do not converge too far.** MAP-Elites keeps the best of *each niche*, not one global best
  (§9). Two families with the same interface but different domains (`domain: legal` vs
  `domain: code`) are not duplicates. They merge only if they pass each other's golden pairs.

### 4.4 Niches and diversity

Biology's lesson, and quality-diversity's, is that selection with a single fitness collapses
diversity and then breaks when the environment changes. Keep a small **elite per niche**:

- niche = `(interface signature, domain label)`;
- the elite = the library family with the best Beta mean in that niche, plus at most one
  challenger (`kept`) that the bandit keeps sampling;
- everything else in the niche decays to `forgotten` on TTL.

Neutral drift is allowed. Variants with no measurable difference can coexist in scratch at no
cost, because only the library is curated.

### 4.5 Types evolve by the same rule

Schemas are selected like tools, and the tiers come from Kubernetes (alpha/beta/GA; §9) and
atproto's naming rules (01-data-model §3.4):

| Tier | Type id | Rule | Promotion trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **local** (alpha) | `p.<problem-ulid>.<name>` | Free-form. Can disappear with its problem. Readers outside the problem see it only as `doc`. | — |
| **shared** (beta) | `<domain>.<name>`, e.g. `research.finding` | Additive changes only. A breaking change takes a new id. A shared type is not removed while any `kept`+ family writes it. | ≥ 2 families in ≥ 2 distinct problems write bodies that are structurally compatible. The system proposes the **union schema** (fields seen in all, with optional fields seen in some), and a human accepts. |
| **standard** (GA) | `std.<name>` (`std.decision`, `std.problem`, `std.outcome`) | Never removed, only deprecated. Envelope-level fields are versioned as a whole (02-B §4.3). | Used across ≥ 2 projects and by ≥ 3 library families, with a human-accepted `decision type: type.tier`. |

Moving from local to shared works through a **lens**, which is an ordinary adapter tool
(`p.01JA3.rec → research.finding`). It is generated, selected and credited like any other tool.
Old records stay in their old type, unmigrated. A reader that wants the new type runs the lens.
This is atproto's rule plus Cambria's lens idea demoted to a module (01-data-model §3.4).

### 4.6 How the standard itself changes

The standard is the envelope, the few kinds, the `std.*` types and the trust rule. It is the only
thing meant to last, so it changes most slowly, and only by **documenting practice that has
already been selected**. Wikipedia works the same way: its policies "document the good practices
accepted in the community" rather than invent them (§9).

- A field enters `std.*` only after it has existed as an optional field in shared types used in
  ≥ 2 projects. The standard never adds a field nobody has used.
- **Additive only.** Readers must ignore unknown fields (open unions, `knownValues` not enums).
- A breaking change is a new type id (`std.decision.v2`) and a lens, never a migration. Old records
  remain valid forever under the version that wrote them.
- The envelope gets a version field only if it ever changes, and it should not (02-B §4.3).
- **The trust rule is not under selection.** Who can make a record authoritative is not a fitness
  question. It changes only by an explicit owner decision, never because a cheaper path "won".

## 5. Chains as selectable things

A tool is one step. Most of the value of "cheap tools" lies in the **combination** that solved a
problem. Chains get the same lifecycle as tools.

### 5.1 From trace to preset

When an outcome is accepted as `solved` or `partial`, a deterministic fold extracts the **chain
trace**: the runs in the problem's lineage, ordered, with the families used and the record types
on each edge. A trace is a fact, not a recipe.

```json
{ "kind": "doc", "type": "chain.trace",
  "links": [ { "rel": "about", "to": "problem:01JA3…" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<outcome>" } ],
  "body": {
    "steps": [
      { "n": 1, "family": "tool/research",          "pin": "sha256:…", "in": ["std.problem"],      "out": ["doc"] },
      { "n": 2, "family": "tool/extract-decisions", "pin": "sha256:…", "in": ["doc"],              "out": ["std.decision:proposed"] },
      { "n": 3, "human": "accept",                                     "in": ["std.decision:proposed"], "out": ["std.decision:accepted"] },
      { "n": 4, "family": "tool/swarm-add-basis",   "pin": "sha256:…", "in": ["std.decision:accepted"], "out": ["artifact"] }
    ],
    "dropped": [ { "family": "tool/kb-index", "reason": "ran, no credit" } ],
    "cost": { "usd": 11.40, "humanActs": 9 }
  } }
```

After **two** traces with the same step skeleton (same families in the same order, ignoring
pins), the system proposes a **preset**. That is Agent Workflow Memory's move, inducing reusable
workflows from successful trajectories (§9), with a human gate:

```json
{ "kind": "doc", "type": "chain.preset",
  "subject": "preset/design-question-to-build",
  "links": [ { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<trace 1>" },
             { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<trace 2>" } ],
  "body": {
    "fits": { "domain": ["design"], "criteriaShape": ["decision-exists", "build-pinned"] },
    "recipe": "research → extract-decisions → [human accept] → swarm add --basis",
    "params": [ { "name": "question", "from": "std.problem.statement" } ],
    "gates": ["accept before swarm"],
    "expected": { "usd": [6, 15], "humanActs": [5, 12] }
  } }
```

`recipe` is the plan text that 04 §5 already describes, shaped in conversation and approved as a
diff. A preset is that text with a track record attached.

### 5.2 Selecting and mutating chains

- **Proposal.** For a new problem the proposer retrieves presets by `fits` (domain labels,
  criteria shape and statement similarity) and shows the top two or three, each with its fitness
  vector: problems solved, cost range, human acts and last use. The human's pick is a `choice`
  record, and it is a selection signal in itself.
- **Mutation is recorded, not hidden.** The agent adapts the preset to the problem and writes the
  adapted recipe `derivedFrom` the preset. The **diff** between preset and adaptation is data. If
  a step is dropped in 3 of 4 adaptations, the system proposes a preset revision without it. If a
  step is added in 3 of 4, it proposes one with it. Evolution by use, in a form that can be
  checked.
- **Chain fitness** uses the same fold as tools (§3.7), at the level of outcomes. A chain's
  credit is simply its outcomes' values, and its blame is its failed outcomes. Cost and
  `humanActs` per solved problem are first-class fields, because a chain that solves problems by
  asking the human 30 times is worse than its success rate suggests.
- **Chains contain chains.** A preset can be a step in another preset. Credit then flows to the
  inner preset as if it were one tool. This is how "if they can be combined, their use grows"
  compounds. A small preset (`spec → critic → owner`) that sits inside many larger ones gets high
  `consumedBy`.

## 6. Context accumulation: what the system learns across problems

Three kinds of things accumulate. All of them are records, and none of them is hidden state. 04 §9
flags long-lived private module state as a covert channel between compositions.

| What | Record | Comes from | Feeds the next problem by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who and what** (people, meetings, sources) | `doc type: contact` / `interaction`, with `origin` (04 §6b) and `about: problem` | Integrations at the edge (mail, calendar, notes), human entry | The proposer reads contacts linked to *similar* past problems: "last time you asked X about storage; X is linked to 2 solved storage problems". |
| **What worked** (tools, chains) | the fitness fold (§3.7), `chain.preset` | §3, §5 | Ranking presets and tools for the new problem. |
| **Lessons** | `claim type: lesson`, proposed by an agent after an outcome and `derivedFrom` that outcome and its trace | A retrospective step at the end of a chain, maybe a preset step itself | The proposer includes accepted lessons whose `fits` overlap the new problem. |

```json
{ "kind": "claim", "type": "lesson", "status": "proposed",
  "links": [ { "rel": "derivedFrom", "to": "sha256:<outcome>" },
             { "rel": "about", "to": "problem:01JA3…" } ],
  "body": { "text": "Research tracks that measure (benchmarks) should not go through decide; they need a run step.",
            "fits": { "domain": ["performance"] },
            "evidence": ["sha256:<friction-log entry>"] } }
```

**The proposer is a module whose reads-set is its explanation.** When it proposes a chain for a
new problem, its `prov.reads` lists exactly which presets, lessons, contacts and fitness snapshots
it looked at. "Why did it suggest this?" is answered by reading provenance, not by trusting an
opaque memory. Any index it uses (embeddings over problem statements, a contact graph) is a
derived view that can be rebuilt from the log (02-B §2.2).

**Lessons are selected too.** A lesson gets credit when a later problem's proposer read it and that
problem was solved. It gets blame when a chain followed it and failed. Lessons that never get
read, or get read and then fail, are forgotten on TTL like scratch tools. Without that, a memory
of lessons turns into a junk drawer that is never cleaned. That is the agent-memory form of the
ClawHub problem.

## 7. What is measurable in S0, from files alone

04's first slice has no kernel. Records are files in git, and `derivedFrom` is self-reported (C's
weakness, see §8). Even so, this much can be measured in the first six weeks, in the style of the
kill table in 04 §6:

| Signal | Promising if | Selection is premature if |
|---|---|---|
| Reuse across problems | ≥ 3 generated tools are used in a second, *unrelated* problem | Every tool is regenerated from scratch each time, and nobody misses the old one |
| Survival is informative | Survival differs between tools (some ≈1, some ≈0.3) and predicts which you reuse | Survival is ≈1 everywhere (rubber-stamping) or ≈0 everywhere (you rewrite everything) |
| Outcome records get written | ≥ 80% of closed problems have an accepted outcome with pruned `derivedFrom` | Outcomes are skipped. **Then nothing in this document can work, and the problem/outcome spine itself needs rethinking** |
| A chain repeats | Two traces share a skeleton, and the proposed preset is used a third time | Every problem's chain is unique |
| Near-duplicates appear | The niche check (§4.1 step 1) finds ≥ 2 families in one niche | Families never collide. Dedup can wait |

If after six weeks fewer than about 10 problems have accepted outcomes, **build none of the
machinery**. Keep `problem`, `outcome` and honest `derivedFrom` as conventions, and let the human
remember which tools were good. Selection needs a population, and the first job is to see whether
one appears.

## 8. Risks and open questions

- **Credit is only as honest as `derivedFrom`.** In B the kernel checks `derivedFrom ⊆ reads`. In
  C/S0 the tool writes its own links, so a tool could cite nothing (and lose credit) or cite
  everything (diluted by fan-in, but not prevented). The minimum without a kernel is for the
  launcher to record the input hashes it handed the tool and for a verifier to check the subset.
  That is 04's D principle (attest at the edges) applied to provenance.
- **The human prunes the outcome's `derivedFrom` list** (§3.6). It is the best credit signal and
  also an extra chore. If it is skipped, credit falls back to the whole lineage, which is weaker
  but still works.
- **Problem distance** (§3.4) needs a notion of related problems. Shared `about` ancestry and
  label overlap are crude. Embedding distance between statements is better but not deterministic.
- **Small N makes every threshold noise.** The numbers in §2.2 are defaults. The Beta posterior
  shows its uncertainty honestly. A hard threshold does not.
- **Promotion fatigue.** If the system proposes too many `tool.tier` decisions, the human
  batch-accepts them, and that undermines the signal (§3.4). Cap promotion proposals at a few a
  week, ranked by the fitness vector.
- **Supply chain.** A library tool is code that runs with your grants. Promotion is the moment to
  review it, and it should show the diff of what the tool can touch (caps, grants), not only its
  scores. The ClawHub audits (§9) are the warning.
- **Unverified:** whether LLM-generated tools actually get reused across *unrelated* problems at
  this scale. ToolLibGen, SkillWeaver and Voyager show reuse within one benchmark domain. None of
  them shows a personal, mixed-domain workload.

## 9. Prior art (what each one contributes here)

**Agent tool and skill libraries**
- **Voyager** (Wang et al., 2023): an ever-growing library of executable code skills, added only
  after self-verification, retrieved by description and composed into new skills. It shows that
  verify-before-commit and composition compound abilities. Its judge is automatic, and ours is the
  human. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291
- **CREATOR / CRAFT** (2023): abstracting reusable functions from solution traces; CRAFT stresses
  "diverse, reusable, correct" toolsets. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14318 ,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17428
- **ToolLibGen** (2025): clusters question-specific tools by topic, a code agent refactors each
  cluster into aggregated tools, and a reviewing agent checks the aggregate keeps the originals'
  functionality. Reports better retrieval as the number of tools grows (abstract only; numbers not
  read). The source of §4.3's merge pattern. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07768
- **ToolScope** (2025) and **El Agente Forjador** (2026): merge by embedding clusters plus a
  validator or verification agent before deleting originals. Secondary summaries only
  *(unverified)*. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20036v1 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14609
- **Agent Workflow Memory** (ICML 2025): induces reusable workflows from successful trajectories
  and supplies them to later tasks (+24.6% / +51.1% relative success on Mind2Web / WebArena). The
  basis of §5.1. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html
- **SkillWeaver** (2025): web agents propose, practice and distill skills into APIs. APIs from
  strong agents raise weak agents' success by up to 54.3%, which shows that a library outlives the
  model that wrote it. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07079
- **Alita** (2025): starts with minimal tools and builds MCP tools on the fly. It is the
  generate-per-problem premise in its purest form *(secondary summary, unverified)*.
- **SMITH / "Joint Optimization of Tool Creation and Use"** (Tam et al., Aug 2026): one RL policy
  both creates and uses tools, with separate reward axes for schema, code and outcome failures.
  The tools it creates also help other models. Separate failure axes match §3's vector fitness.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24571
- **Darwin Gödel Machine** (Zhang et al., 2025): keeps an *archive* of agent variants, samples
  parents from it and keeps "interesting" ones rather than only the best (SWE-bench 20% → 50%).
  Archive plus open-endedness is the opposite of a single leaderboard. Its "ICLR 2026" venue comes
  from a secondary page *(unverified)*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954
- **Agent Skills** (Anthropic, SKILL.md): name and description loaded first, full body on demand.
  This is progressive disclosure, so a big library costs little context. The "open standard,
  Dec 2025, adopted by OpenAI/Google/GitHub/Cursor" claim comes from secondary articles
  *(unverified)*. https://www.newsletter.swirlai.com/p/agent-skills-progressive-disclosure

**Package ecosystems and stores (mostly warnings)**
- **npms.io** scores npm packages as quality 0.30, popularity 0.35, maintenance 0.35. Popularity is
  mostly downloads and stars. It shows that even a careful registry leans on popularity.
  https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~wshang/pubs/npms.pdf *(score weights via search summary)*
- **Trivial packages** (Abdalkareem et al.): 10–16% of npm/PyPI packages are trivial, and
  developers assume they are well tested, but only ~28% (npm) have tests. Adoption is not
  evidence of quality.
  https://gitea.communitydata.science/mgaughan/adaptation-slr/raw/branch/master/studies/023_abdalkareem.pdf
- **ClawHub / skills.sh audits** (Feb–May 2026): reported 7.6–13.4% of agent skills malicious or
  critically flawed. Blog sources *(unverified figures)*.
  https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/07/agent-skill-supply-chain-crisis-clawhavoc-toxicskills-skillsieve-codex-cli-defence/

**Governance and evolution rules**
- **Debian britney**: unstable → testing migration requires an age and no RC-bug regressions. The
  model for §2.2. Cited via the Lubuntu fork of britney2 (its README lists the age and RC-bug
  policies). https://git.lubuntu.me/Lubuntu/britney2-ubuntu
- **Kubernetes deprecation policy**: alpha can vanish any release, beta has bounded lifetimes, GA
  is deprecated but never removed within a major version, and a version may not be replaced by a
  less stable one. The model for §2.1 and §4.5.
  https://kubernetes.io/docs/reference/using-api/deprecation-policy/
- **atproto lexicons / Cambria lenses**: additive evolution, a new id for breaking changes, lenses
  as translators (01-data-model §2.7, §3.4).
- **Wikipedia policies and guidelines**: policies document accepted practice and reflect
  consensus. The model for §4.6. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Policies_and_guidelines

**Evolution and selection theory**
- **MAP-Elites / quality diversity** (Mouret & Clune, 2015): keep the elite of each niche, not one
  global best. Diversity also tends to find better overall solutions. The basis of §4.4.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909
- **Goodhart's / Campbell's law**: a measure that is a target is corrupted *(background)*.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law
- **Thompson sampling / Bradley-Terry**: standard small-sample bandit and pairwise-preference
  methods *(background, not re-checked)*.
- **Biological analogies used**:
  - selection acts on the phenotype, so tools are judged by their outputs, not their code;
  - exaptation is reuse in an unrelated problem, weighted highest;
  - neutral drift lets scratch variants coexist at no cost;
  - horizontal gene transfer is merging;
  - apoptosis is forgetting from the index, not deleting.

## What is cable, jack and patch here

- **Cable:** unchanged. Records flow by hash. Selection adds no new cable. It *reads* the cables
  that were used: `derivedFrom` edges are the cables that actually carried value, and the credit
  walk (§3.2) runs backwards along them.
- **Jack:** the **niche** is the jack signature (`reads → writes` types). Tools compete only
  within a jack signature. Promoting a type (local → shared → std) is how a new jack kind becomes
  available to everything. Combinability (§3.8) measures how many cables a jack has carried.
- **Patch:** a `chain.preset` is a patch with a track record. It is selected like a tool and
  mutated with a recorded diff, and as an accepted plan text it is still subject to 04's rule that
  the patch is the plan, not the authority.
- **Sonic contrast:** in Sonic you *hear* whether a patch is good at once. Here the "hearing" is
  the outcome verdict, days later and judged by a human. That is why selection needs the problem
  record and outcome as a spine, and why it cannot run on counts.

## Implications for the kernel

**The kernel adds nothing for selection** except properties of records it already owns:

1. **Honest `derivedFrom`.** Credit depends on it. B's check (`derivedFrom ⊆ reads-set`), or a
   launcher-recorded input list in C/S0, belongs at the trust edge, because without it fitness can
   be forged.
2. **`about: problem` propagates through lineage**, like B's labels inherit from the trigger, so
   every run can be attributed to a problem without the tool's cooperation.
3. **`outcome` is a human-authoritative type**, under the same rule as `decision`: an agent drafts
   it, and a human accepts it over exact hashes.
4. **Promotion, merge, deprecation and type promotion are `decision` records**, not new verbs. The
   closed authority vocabulary stays closed.
5. **Nothing is ever deleted.** "Forgotten" and "deprecated" are index states. Old hashes stay
   resolvable and runnable, and old records are never migrated.

**Modules, not kernel:**
- the fitness fold;
- the golden-pair fold;
- the niche and dedup finder;
- the trace extractor;
- the preset proposer;
- the lesson retrospective;
- merges.

All of them are rebuildable from the log. The folds are deterministic and $0, so they may run
unattended (04 §8.4). Everything that costs money (shadow runs, behavioral dedup, LLM merges) is a
budgeted proposal.
